\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Incremental nonlinear dynamic inversion (INDI) computes a small change of the actuator command from the measured angular or linear acceleration.
It needs few model parameters and rejects disturbances without an estimator or an integrator.
This report presents two INDI controllers for PX4 multicopters.
The inner controller replaces the angular-rate controller, and the outer controller replaces the position controller's thrust step with a linear-acceleration loop.
Both use the rotor speed that bidirectional DShot telemetry reports.
They are selected by parameter, work in every flight mode, and leave the stock PID controllers untouched and as the default.

The report states the problem, derives the controllers, gives the stability analysis and lists the evidence.
All tests run in Gazebo software-in-the-loop simulation on one 5-inch quadcopter of about 600~g.
Eight acceptance criteria were fixed before the tests.
Six are met: evidence integrity, stability margins, noise and effort, modes and transitions, code and upstream readiness, and this report.
Measured in flight, the outer loops keep a phase margin of at least 49.1 deg and the rate loop was checked at 46.9 deg, against a limit of 45 deg.

Two criteria are not met.
The 300-run randomised robustness campaign shows INDI-specific failures: a sustained 2~Hz pitch oscillation with slow motors, and a loss of control at a saturated, weak-motor corner where PID flies on.
The tuning method, which depends on that campaign, therefore also fails.
The report ends with a bring-up checklist for real hardware.
