\chapter{Inner Loop: Angular-Rate Control}\label{ch:inner_loop}

This chapter describes the rate controller.
It is the fast inner loop of the cascade.
It takes a body-rate setpoint and returns a normalised torque setpoint for the control allocator.
The chapter goes from the problem, to the idea, to the design, and ends with the parameters a user can set.
The outer loop follows in \cref{ch:outer_loop}.
The margins of the finished loop are analysed in \cref{ch:analysis}.

\section{Problem and idea}\label{sec:inner_problem}

The stock PX4 rate controller is a PID law.
It needs gains that match the airframe, and it reacts to a disturbance only after the rate error has grown.
A weak motor or a sagging battery changes the plant, and the gains no longer fit.

The INDI law of \cref{sec:indi_principle} avoids this.
It adds an increment to the torque that is already acting.
The increment is the torque needed to turn the measured angular acceleration $\dot\omega_f$ into the desired one.
The law needs the control effectiveness $G_1$, and it needs the torque that is acting now.
Three questions follow.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item How is the desired angular acceleration $\nu$ formed from the rate setpoint?
  \item What is the torque that is acting now, and how is it measured?
  \item How do the two signals, $\dot\omega_f$ and that torque, stay in step?
\end{enumerate}
The sections below answer them in this order.

\paragraph{Placement.}
The rate module runs instead of the stock rate controller when \param{MC_INDI_EN} is 1 (reboot required).
Only one rate controller runs.
The module refuses to start if another one already publishes the torque setpoint.
There is no PID fallback inside the module.
It runs on every sample of the angular-velocity topic, so at the rate of the gyroscope chain.
The simulation tests used 800, 400 and 266.7~Hz.

\Cref{tab:inner_io} lists the signals.
All quantities are per axis and scalar, in normalised torque space.
Here $u$ is the normalised torque of one axis, and $G_1$ is the diagonal entry of the effectiveness matrix for that axis.
Torque lies in $[-1,\,1]$, and $G_1$ is in rad/s$^2$ per unit of normalised torque.

\section{The rate law}\label{sec:inner_law}

\subsection{Desired acceleration and setpoint lead}

The desired angular acceleration is proportional to the rate error:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner_nu}
  \nu = K\,(\omega_{\mathrm{ref}} - \omega) .
\end{equation}
For roll and pitch, $\omega_{\mathrm{ref}}$ includes the feed-forward from the outer loop.
The gain $K$ is the rate bandwidth, in rad/s per rad/s, that is in 1/s.
Until the physical parameters are marked as identified (\param{MC_INDI_ID_OK}), the loop uses half of the set value.
This halves the bandwidth of an unidentified airframe.
In this chapter $K$ means the effective gain, so it is already halved while \param{MC_INDI_ID_OK} is 0.

The torque $u_f$ in the feedback pair carries the actuator lag.
A moving setpoint would therefore be followed late.
A lead term removes this.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner_lead}
  c = \min\!\bigl(\ell\,K\,\tau_a,\; 2\bigr) ,
\end{equation}
where $\tau_a$ is the actuator time constant (\param{MC_INDI_ACT_TC}) and $\ell$ is the lead scale (\param{MC_INDI_LEAD}).
The weight $(1+c)$ on the measured acceleration below, and the extra term $c\,\dot\omega_{\mathrm{ref},f}$, come from the lag.
The torque $u_f$ trails the command by about $\tau_a$, so these terms compensate for this known lag.
Let $\dot\omega_{\mathrm{ref},f}$ be the derivative of the rate setpoint, low-pass filtered with a time constant of 0.03~s and bounded to 1000~rad/s$^2$.
With the lead, the effective desired acceleration is $K(1+\tau_a s)(\omega_{\mathrm{ref}}-\omega)$, for $\ell=1$ and $c<2$.
Setting $\ell=0$ turns both lead terms off.
The setpoint derivative is zero in the first cycle, after a reset, after a change of the setpoint source, and on the ground.
So no derivative impulse enters the law.

\subsection{Inversion}

For roll and pitch the increment is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner_inc}
  \Delta u = \frac{\nu - (1+c)\,\dot\omega_f + c\,\dot\omega_{\mathrm{ref},f}}{G_1} ,
\end{equation}
and the torque command is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner_cmd}
  u_c = \operatorname{clip}\bigl(u_f + \operatorname{clip}(\Delta u,\,\pm\Delta_{\max}),\; -1,\; 1\bigr) .
\end{equation}
Here $u_f$ is the feedback torque after the ground handling of \cref{sec:inner_ground}.
The bound $\Delta_{\max}$ is \param{MC_INDI_INC_MAX}.
Its default of 1 is the full range, so the bound is inactive.
Both $\dot\omega_f$ and $u_f$ pass through the same filter chain $H$ (\cref{sec:inner_filters}).
If the plant follows $\dot\omega = G_1 u$, the two cancel.
Then $u_c$ is the previous torque plus the increment that produces $\nu$.

\begin{keyresult}[No integrator, no disturbance estimator]
The difference between $\dot\omega_f$ and the torque $u_f$ that produced it is what the airframe did not do as commanded.
The law reacts to this difference directly.
It has no integrator, so it has no windup state.
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{Yaw}

Yaw torque comes from a change of rotor speed.
The rotor inertia reacts on the body.
For yaw, the plant on the increment is $(G_1 + G_2 s)\,\Delta u = \nu - (1+c)\dot\omega_f$.
The law inverts it with a backward-Euler step:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner_yaw}
  \Bigl(G_1 + \frac{G_2}{\ts}\Bigr)\Delta u
  = \nu - (1+c)\,\dot\omega_f + c\,\dot\omega_{\mathrm{ref},f} + \frac{G_2}{\ts}\,\Delta u_{\mathrm{prev}} .
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta u_{\mathrm{prev}}$ is the increment of the previous cycle that reached the output after clipping.
Using it in this way is a back-calculation: if the output is clipped, the next increment starts from what was applied.
\Cref{eq:inner_yaw} is a first-order low pass on the increment.
Its DC gain is $1/G_1$ and its time constant is $G_2/G_1$.
Both are independent of the sample period.
This time constant is not the motor time constant.
With $G_2=0$ the equation becomes \cref{eq:inner_inc}.
For the reference airframe, $G_2$ follows from the rotor inertia (\param{MC_INDI_G2_Y}).

An optional second-order low pass can be placed on the yaw channel (\param{MC_INDI_YAW_CUT}, in Hz).
It acts on the yaw components of $\dot\omega_f$, $u_f$, the yaw rate error and the yaw setpoint, with identical coefficients.
So the pair stays in step.
It is off at 0, and also off above 0.45 times the sample rate.
Roll and pitch are never filtered this way.
The default is off.

\section{The actuator state}\label{sec:inner_actuator}

The torque $u_f$ is what the rotors did, not what was commanded.
Two sources give it.

\paragraph{Modelled torque.}
A first-order actuator model is driven by the torque the allocator actually produced from the previous setpoint:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner_model}
  u_{\mathrm{act}}[k] = u_{\mathrm{act}}[k-1] + \bigl(1 - e^{-\ts/\tau_a}\bigr)\bigl(u_{\mathrm{alloc}}[k] - u_{\mathrm{act}}[k-1]\bigr) .
\end{equation}
The allocated torque is the previous setpoint minus the unallocated torque reported by the allocator.
So the model sees saturation.
The time constant $\tau_a$ is limited to $[0.001,\,0.2]$~s.
The model output is delayed by \param{MC_INDI_DELAY} samples (at most 4) and then passed through the filter chain $H$, giving $u_{\mathrm{mod},f}$.

\paragraph{Measured torque.}
The speed controllers report the rotor speed $\Omega_i$.
The normalised rotor state is $u_i=(\Omega_i/\Omax)^2$, as in \cref{eq:u_state}, clipped to $[0,1]$ for each motor.
The speed $\Omax$ is \param{MC_INDI_W_MAX}.
The measured torque follows from the effectiveness matrix $B$ of the control allocation library:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner_meas}
  u_{\mathrm{meas}} = B\,(\vect{u}_{\mathrm{rot}} - \vect{u}_{\mathrm{trim}}) .
\end{equation}
Here $\vect{u}_{\mathrm{rot}}$ collects the rotor states $u_i$, and $\vect{u}_{\mathrm{trim}}$ is the trim state.
$B$ is built from the \param{CA_*} rotor geometry and scaled in the same way as the allocator scales it.
So the measured torque uses the same geometry as the allocator, with no extra parameter per axis.
Rows of $B$ with all entries below 0.05 are set to zero, as in the allocator.
The measured path has its own delay (\param{MC_INDI_ERPM_DLY}) and its own copy of the filter chain $H$.
The result is $u_{\mathrm{meas},f}$.

\param{MC_INDI_W_MAX} must be set.
With its default of 0 the rate module refuses to arm, because no measured torque can be built.
An invalid rotor geometry also gives no measured torque.

\paragraph{Switching.}
The measurement replaces the model only after 20 consecutive valid cycles.
Any invalid cycle drops the law back to the model at once.
The switch is made for all axes together, because the model and the measurement differ in lag.
The blend below then corrects the slow difference between them.
While the measurement is invalid, its path is fed the modelled torque.
So the switch back is without a step.

\section{Filters and filter matching}\label{sec:inner_filters}

The angular acceleration $\dot\omega_f$ comes from the sensor chain.
This chain is a second-order low pass (\param{IMU_GYRO_CUTOFF}) followed by a first-order low pass on the derivative (\param{IMU_DGYRO_CUTOFF}).
The torque $u_f$ must see the same lag.
Otherwise the difference $\nu-(1+c)\dot\omega_f$ carries a filter artefact instead of the true gap.

The library therefore builds the same chain $H$ on the torque.
The chain is a second-order low pass followed by the derivative filter, with the cutoffs of the sensor chain.
A cutoff at or below 0 turns that stage into a pass-through, as in the sensor chain.
The filters are designed on a low-passed sample rate.
They are redesigned only when this rate moves by more than 10~\% or a cutoff changes.
So jitter of the sample period does not reset the pair.
A change of the delay in flight moves one sample per cycle.

\begin{indinote}[Matching is part of the design]
If the filter cutoffs or the delay of the modelled torque are changed without care, the pair is no longer matched.
The delay default of 0 was computed for the filter cutoffs of the bench configuration.
For other cutoffs it is not verified.
\end{indinote}

\section{Removing the steady command/output gap}\label{sec:inner_gap}

A motor can deliver less than it is commanded.
Causes are a low battery, a weak motor and a thrust-map error.
The result is a steady gap between the command and the output.

With the measured torque alone, the gap is invisible as an error that persists.
The law sees what the motor really does, and treats it as the new normal.
In a simulated closed-loop test, one motor that delivered 80~\% of its command then held a 10.4~deg standing attitude error.

The model does see the gap, because it follows the command through the allocator.
The library therefore blends the two sources.
The measurement carries the high frequencies, and the model carries the low ones.
Let $b$ be the blend state per axis.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner_blend}
  \dot b = \frac{u_{\mathrm{mod},f} - u_{\mathrm{meas},f} - b}{t_b} ,
  \qquad u_f = u_{\mathrm{meas},f} + b .
\end{equation}
The time constant $t_b$ is \param{MC_INDI_ACT_BLD}.
The blend state is bounded to $|b|\le 2$ per axis.
In discrete time the update is $b \leftarrow b + \tfrac{\ts}{t_b+\ts}\,(u_{\mathrm{mod},f}-u_{\mathrm{meas},f}-b)$.

A steady shortfall of the motor appears as $u_{\mathrm{mod},f}-u_{\mathrm{meas},f}$.
The state $b$ carries it at DC.
At high frequency, $b$ moves slowly, so $u_f\approx u_{\mathrm{meas},f}$.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The state $b$ follows the gap at once, without the time constant, whenever the measurement is not in use.
    This holds also on the ground.
    Every switch is then without a step.
  \item The state $b$ is held while the vehicle is flagged as maybe landed.
  \item With $t_b\le 0$ the blend is off and the measured torque is used alone.
\end{itemize}

The default $t_b=1.0$~s gives a blend corner of $1/(2\pi t_b)=0.16$~Hz.
This lies far below the rate crossover, which is 1.7~Hz in the measured loop of \cref{sec:inner_margins}.
So it does not move the margins.
In a simulated closed-loop unit test with one weak motor, $t_b=0$ left a standing rate error of 0.25~rad/s.
With $t_b=1$ it was $1.6\times10^{-5}$~rad/s.

\section{Saturation}\label{sec:inner_sat}

The law has no integrator, so there is no windup state.
Saturation enters in three places.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The increment is bounded to $\Delta_{\max}$ per cycle and the output to $[-1,\,1]$.
  \item The actuator model is fed with the torque the allocator produced, not with the setpoint.
    So the model state, and through the blend the feedback pair, see the clipped torque.
  \item The measured torque is the real output, so it shows saturation directly.
\end{itemize}
The priority between axes and thrust, when the allocator saturates, is set by the stock control allocator.
This design does not change it.
The module only reports the unallocated torque back into the law.
The limits of this design under saturation with a weak motor are in \cref{ch:limits}.

\section{Ground handling}\label{sec:inner_ground}

On the ground the measured acceleration says nothing about the torque.
The rotors may be idle, and the vehicle rests on its legs.
The law therefore treats the ground as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Landed.}
    The feedback torque $u_f$ in \cref{eq:inner_cmd} is held at 0.
    The lead gain $c$ and the setpoint derivative are 0.
    The yaw increment state is 0.
    The blend state follows the gap.
    The actuator model and both filter chains keep running, so the pair is in step at take-off.
  \item \emph{Edges of landed.}
    On both edges, the feedback torque is ramped with the slew \param{MC_INDI_LND_SLEW} (5 per second, so a change of 1 takes 0.2~s).
    It is not stepped.
  \item \emph{Maybe landed.}
    The law runs in full.
    Only the blend state is held.
    There is no output slew limit in flight.
  \item \emph{Disarmed.}
    The library is reset in every cycle.
    The blend and the filters restart at arming.
\end{itemize}

\section{Guards}\label{sec:inner_guards}

\paragraph{Rotor telemetry.}
The measured torque is valid only if every rotor has a fresh, finite and plausible sample.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The oldest sample is younger than \param{MC_INDI_ERPM_TO} (0.02~s).
  \item The motor is online, and its error counter did not change.
    A dropped frame skips the sample.
  \item The speed is not negative, and $\Omega/\Omax\le 1.5$.
  \item The value is not stuck (below).
\end{itemize}
One invalid motor makes the whole torque invalid.
The law then uses the modelled path within one cycle.

\paragraph{Stuck rotor speed.}
A motor is latched as stuck when its reported speed is bit-identical for \param{MC_INDI_ERPM_FRZ} (0.5~s) while armed, and its command varied by more than 0.02 in the same window.
It is treated as invalid until the speed changes.
A constant command only restarts the window, because a constant speed is then legitimate.
The detection restarts at arming and disarming.
The masks of valid and stuck motors are published in \topic{actuator_torque_measured}.

\paragraph{Arming.}
The rate module registers an external arming check with the commander.
Arming is refused in two cases.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Any rotor has no fresh, plausible and non-stuck sample, or the oldest sample is older than \param{MC_INDI_ERPM_TO}.
  \item The measured path is not configured: \param{MC_INDI_W_MAX} is 0, or the \param{CA_*} rotor geometry is missing.
\end{itemize}
Once armed, the check always allows.
It has no effect in flight.
Telemetry lost in flight is handled by the modelled path, without the blend.
This fallback is not validated for the whole flight envelope (\cref{ch:limits}).

\paragraph{Rejected cycles.}
An input that is not finite, a sample period outside $[0.000125,\,0.02]$~s, an effectiveness below 0.001, a parameter that is not finite or out of range, or a non-finite increment makes the update fail.
The last valid torque is held for 3 cycles.
Then the states are reset and the torque is zero.
A non-finite increment resets the states at once, and then the last torque is held.

\paragraph{Setpoint timeout.}
When armed in a mode that uses the rate setpoint, a setpoint older than \param{MC_INDI_SP_TO} (0.5~s) is stale.
The rates are then set to zero, with the thrust held.
A warning is emitted at most every 2~s.
The feed-forward from the outer loop is applied only if it is valid, younger than 50~ms, not on the ground, not in acro mode and not stale.

\section{Parameters}\label{sec:inner_params}

\Cref{tab:inner_params} lists the parameters of the rate module.
The column \emph{Reference} gives the value used on the simulated reference airframe.
The column \emph{Default} gives the firmware default.
The reference airframe values of $G_1$ and $G_2$ come from airframe data and are not identified in flight.
The firmware defaults for $G_1$ and $G_2$ equal them.
This is on purpose: a value of $G_1$ above the true one lowers the loop gain, so it is the safe side for a larger airframe.
A lower default, for example 320 and 210~rad/s$^2$ for roll and pitch, would raise the loop gain on this airframe by a factor of 5 to 8.

The table lists 21 parameters: 20 for tuning and one enable switch.
Nine more parameters (\param{MC_INDI_INJ_*}) drive a bench excitation for loop-break measurements.
They are off by default and are not part of the flight design.
The loop also reads \param{IMU_GYRO_CUTOFF}, \param{IMU_DGYRO_CUTOFF} and the \param{CA_*} rotor geometry.
These belong to other modules.

\paragraph{Effective bandwidth.}
With \param{MC_INDI_ID_OK} at 0, which is the default, the loop runs at $K=10$~1/s, half of the set 20~1/s.
Set it to 1 to use the full gains.

\paragraph{Actuator time constant.}
The default of \param{MC_INDI_ACT_TC} is 0.05~s for an unidentified airframe.
The reference airframe uses 0.033~s, which is the time constant of its simulated motor model.
It is the value the identification step returns for this airframe.

\paragraph{Library constants.}
Some values are fixed in the library: 20 valid cycles to switch to the measurement, 3 hold cycles on a fault, a blend bound of 2, a lead bound of 2, a setpoint-derivative time constant of 0.03~s and at most 4 delay samples.

\section{Rate-loop margins}\label{sec:inner_margins}

The analysis chapter treats the margins of all loops (\cref{ch:analysis}).
\Cref{fig:bode_rate} shows the measured response of the roll rate loop.
The loop crosses 0~dB at 1.72~Hz with a phase margin of 46.9~deg.
The gain margin is 15.7~dB at 18.3~Hz, and the delay margin is 75.5~ms.
Pitch and yaw rate margins were not measured in flight.
The model-only fallback has no measured margin either.
