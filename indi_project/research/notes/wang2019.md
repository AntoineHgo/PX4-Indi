## Summary

1. The paper rederives INDI without the time-scale separation argument used in earlier INDI papers, for systems of arbitrary relative degree, and treats the internal dynamics explicitly (Sec. I, II; p. 1116 abstract, p. 1118).
2. With perturbation term δ(z;Δt) from the sampling interval Δt, the closed-loop state is proven ultimately bounded by a class-K function of the perturbation bound δ_ε (Theorem 1, p. 1119), with a conditional local version when the zero dynamics are only locally exponentially stable (Theorem 2, p. 1120).
3. Disturbances enter the external (controlled) states only through their increments Δd, so their effect shrinks as Δt shrinks, but the internal-state bound has a term that does not shrink with Δt (Proposition 1 and Corollary 1, pp. 1121-1122).
4. Model error in α(x) and B(x): the NDI perturbation ε_ndi contains (α − α̂) and (B − B̂)u_ndi, which does not depend on Δt; the INDI perturbation ε_indi contains only δ(z;Δt) and (B − B̂)Δu_indi, which is reduced by a small sampling interval (Sec. III.B.2, pp. 1122-1123). The paper says INDI is "less sensitive" but does not claim independence from B.
5. Numerical support is a single rigid-aircraft gust-load case with actuator dynamics (Sec. IV): with B̂ = 1.3B, INDI's RMS errors are at least 3x smaller than NDI's (Table 1, p. 1126). A 1000-sample Monte Carlo has 31 NDI failures and 0 INDI failures (p. 1128). The paper gives no numeric sampling-time or delay bound that transfers directly to a multirotor rate loop.

## (a) Stability and robustness results, stated precisely

**Setting and assumptions (Sec. II, III.A; pp. 1117-1119).**
- Square, control-affine system ẋ = f(x) + G(x)u, y = h(x), with p = m (p. 1117). B(x) is assumed nonsingular (p. 1117).
- Output relation y^ρ = α(x) + B(x)u (Eq. 5, p. 1117). Relative degree ρ = Σρ_i ≤ n (Eq. 3). If ρ < n there are internal dynamics η̇ = f_0(η, ξ) (Eq. 8, p. 1117).
- INDI (regulation/input-output form), first-order Taylor expansion about the previous sample (p. 1118): y^ρ ≈ y^ρ_0 + B(x_0)Δu + δ(z;Δt) (Eq. 10) [reconstructed]. Here δ(z;Δt) collects the second and higher Taylor terms, of order ‖Δx‖ (Eq. 11). INDI law: Δu = B^{-1}(x_0)(ν − y^ρ_0) (Eq. 12). Closed loop: y^ρ = ν + δ(z;Δt) (Eq. 13), i.e., ξ̇ = A_c ξ + B_c ν + B_c δ(z;Δt).
- Pseudocontrol ν = −Kξ with A_c − B_c K Hurwitz (p. 1119). Lyapunov equation (A_c − B_c K)^T P + P(A_c − B_c K) = −I (p. 1119, text "PAc − Bc K ... −I") [reconstructed].
- Standing assumptions (Sec. III.A, p. 1119): ideal actuators and perfect sensing; partial derivatives of α and B bounded to any order; x continuous (Eqs. 27-28 context).

**Theorem 1 (p. 1119).** If ‖δ(z;Δt)‖₂ ≤ δ_ε for all z ∈ ℝⁿ, and η̇ = f_0(η, ξ) is input-to-state stable (ISS), then the state z is globally ultimately bounded by a class-K function of δ_ε.
- Proof ingredients: V_ξ = ξ^T P ξ with bounds α1‖ξ‖² ≤ V ≤ α2‖ξ‖² (Eq. 29). Derivative bound V̇ ≤ −θ1‖ξ‖² for ‖ξ‖ ≥ μ1 δ_ε, with μ1 = 2‖PB_c‖₂/(1−θ1) and θ1 ∈ (0,1) (Eq. 30) [μ1 reconstructed from text].
- External-state ultimate bound (Eq. 31): ‖ξ‖ ≤ Γ_δε = α1^{-1}α2(μ1 δ_ε). For the quadratic Lyapunov function this is √(λ_max(P)/λ_min(P)) · μ1 δ_ε [reconstructed; the √ is lost in extraction and follows from the definitions of α1, α2 in Eq. 29].
- Internal state: ‖η(t)‖ ≤ β0(‖η(t0)‖, t − t0) + γ0(sup ‖ξ‖) (Eq. 32, p. 1120), so z = (η, ξ) is ultimately bounded by a class-K function of δ_ε (Eq. 33).
- Theorem 1 imposes no restriction on the initial state or on the size of δ_ε (p. 1120).

**Lemmas 1-2 (p. 1119), cited from Khalil [2].**
- Lemma 1: the origin of the closed loop is asymptotically stable if the zero dynamics η̇ = f_0(η, 0) are asymptotically stable. The paper defines "minimum phase" as asymptotically stable zero dynamics.
- Lemma 2: the origin is globally asymptotically stable if η̇ = f_0(η, ξ) is ISS.

**Theorem 2 (p. 1120).** If ‖δ(z;Δt)‖₂ ≤ δ_ε for all z, and the origin of η̇ = f_0(η, 0) is exponentially stable, then there exist a neighborhood D_z of z = 0 and ε* > 0 such that for every z_0 ∈ D_z and δ_ε < ε*, z is ultimately bounded by a class-K function of δ_ε.
- ε* (Eq. 43) [reconstructed]: ε* = (1/μ1) · α2^{-1}( α1( (1/θ5) · α20^{-1}( α10(r) ) ) ), where α10, α20 are the class-K∞ bounds on the Lyapunov function V2(η) (Eq. 35) and r is the radius of the region where exponential stability holds. Extraction is garbled; check the page image before quoting ε*.
- Restrictions (Eq. 41): μ < α20^{-1}α10(r) and ‖η(t0)‖ ≤ α20^{-1}α10(r), where μ bounds the ξ-term in the internal dynamics (Eq. 38: μ = θ5 · sup‖ξ‖ with θ5 = c4 L/(c3 θ3)).
- Internal-state bound (Eq. 42): ‖η(t)‖ ≤ β00(‖η(t0)‖, t − t0) + α10^{-1}α20(μ).
- Ultimate bound (Eq. 45): combines θ6 δ_ε with the α-terms in μ1 δ_ε.

**Disturbances (Sec. II.C and III.B.1; pp. 1118, 1120-1122).**
- Disturbed plant ẋ = f(x) + G(x)u + d, y = Hx − x̄ (Eq. 20, p. 1118). External states ξ_i = h_i(x) − x̄_i with dynamics ẏ = ξ̇ = f_ξ + G_ξ u + H d (Eq. 21). Control law Δu = G_ξ^{-1}(ξ_0)(ν − ξ̇_0) (p. 1118).
- The disturbance enters the external dynamics only through its increment: ξ̇ = ν + HΔd + δ_ξ(ξ;Δt) (Eq. 23, p. 1118; Eq. 48, p. 1121). It enters the internal dynamics directly (Eq. 24) (p. 1119).
- Definitions (Eqs. 46-47, p. 1121): d̄ = sup_t ‖d(t)‖₂ and d_ε(Δt) = sup_t ‖Δd(t)‖₂.
- Proposition 1 (p. 1121). If ‖δ_ξ‖ ≤ δ_ε, η̇ = f_d(η, ξ, d) is C¹ and globally Lipschitz in (η, ξ, d), and the origin of η̇ = f_d(η, 0, 0) is globally exponentially stable, then ξ is globally ultimately bounded by a class-K function of (δ_ε, d_ε), and η is globally ultimately bounded by a class-K function of (d̄, δ_ε, d_ε).
- The external bound is Γ_ξ = α1^{-1}α2(μ2(δ_ε + d_ε)) (Eq. 51), with μ2 = 2‖P‖₂/(1−θ1) [reconstructed] (Eq. 50).
- Corollary 1 (p. 1121). If the origin of η̇ = f_0(η, 0, 0) is exponentially stable, then there is a neighborhood D_z and ε* > 0 such that, for z_0 ∈ D_z and δ_ε + d_ε < ε*, ξ is ultimately bounded by a class-K function of (δ_ε, d_ε) and η by a class-K function of (d̄, δ_ε, d_ε). Eq. 54 gives the internal bound as a sum of (i) a term θ7 · d̄ that does not depend on Δt and (ii) a term Γ_η = θ5 α10^{-1}α20(Γ_ξ) [reconstructed] that does.

**Model uncertainty (Sec. III.B.2; pp. 1122-1123).**
- NDI (Eq. 55): u_ndi = B^{-1}(x)(−K T2(x) − α(x)) [reconstructed], with ξ = y = Hx and T2 the second block of the transformation T(x) = (η, ξ) (Eq. 7).
- Estimated-model NDI (Eq. 56): same with α̂, B̂.
- INDI (Eq. 58): Δu_indi = B^{-1}(x_0)(−K T2(x) − y^ρ_0). Estimated-model version (Eq. 59): Δu_indi = B̂^{-1}(x_0)(−K T̂2 x − y^ρ_0).
- Closed-loop perturbations: NDI ε_ndi (Eq. 57) = (α − α̂) terms plus (B B̂^{-1} − I) terms; INDI ε_indi (Eq. 60) = δ(z;Δt) plus a term that reduces to (B − B̂)Δu_indi (Eq. 61) [reconstructed].
- Key identity (Eq. 62, p. 1122): the NDI mismatch term (B − B̂)u_ndi depends on the entire control u_ndi and "is independent of Δt". The INDI mismatch term (B − B̂)Δu_indi "can be reduced by increasing the sampling rate" because Δu_indi is an increment (p. 1122).
- Corollary 2 (p. 1122). If ‖ε_ndi/indi‖ ≤ ε̄_ndi/indi for all z and η̇ = f_0 is ISS, then z is globally ultimately bounded by a class-K function of ε̄_ndi and ε̄_indi respectively.
- Corollary 3 (p. 1122). Same with only exponential stability of η̇ = f_0(η, 0), giving a local result under ε_ndi/indi < ε*.

**Singular perturbations and lag (Sec. III.B.3; p. 1123).**
- Tikhonov-type statement (Sec. III.B.3): if the reduced and boundary-layer dynamics are both exponentially stable, there exists ε_max > 0 such that the null equilibrium is exponentially stable for all ε < ε_max. The paper calls ε_max the singular perturbation margin, "equivalent to the phase margin of linear time-invariant systems in the sense of the bijective function" (ref. [26]).
- The paper's conclusion on lag: the system "is able to sustain sufficiently small lags caused by filtering and actuator dynamics", and enlarging the singular perturbation margin is stated as an open research question (p. 1123). No numeric margin or delay bound is given.

## (b) "Model-independent" claim, sampling interval, and actuator dynamics

- **The term "model-independent" does not appear in the paper.** A search of the extracted text for "model-independ" returns nothing. The paper's wording is "requires less model information in both qualitative and quantitative senses, thus improving the system robustness against model uncertainties" (Introduction, p. 1116), and "the INDI control law is less sensitive to model mismatches because α(x) is not used in Eq. (12)" (p. 1118).
- **INDI still needs B.** The INDI law uses B̂ and the measurement or estimate of y^ρ_0 and of the actuator position u_0. The paper calls this "sensor-based" (p. 1118). In the simulation section, "INDI control only needs the estimated control effectiveness matrix B̂, whereas NDI requires both α̂ and B̂" (p. 1127).
- **Prior claim the paper rejects.** The paper says earlier block-diagram analysis [10] showed that mismatch in G(x) or B(x) "has no influence on the closed-loop system", and that this result rests on "the assumption of ẋ = ẋ0 ... incorrect because otherwise there will be no Δu term" (p. 1122).
- **Sampling interval.** δ(z;Δt) and d_ε(Δt) go to zero as Δt → 0 (Eq. 28; Eq. 47). The paper notes that the time-scale-separation argument used in earlier INDI work (dropping δ(z;Δt) because Δz is smaller than the Δu term at high sample rate) "is not mathematically rigorous and is especially deficient for unstable nonlinear plants" (Remark after Eq. 30, p. 1119).
- **Sampling-rate guidance given in the paper (p. 1121).** "for rigid airplane control, normally f_s = 100 Hz is enough [7,10,12,15,18-20], whereas f_s = 1000 Hz is needed for flexible aircraft control [21]. f_s = 512 Hz is used in Ref. [13,14] for quadrotor flight control. For the applications on hydraulic systems, f_s ≈ 5000 Hz is desirable." These are the paper's citations and are not checked here.
- **Actuator dynamics.** The proofs assume ideal actuators (p. 1119). Actuator dynamics and limits are treated only in simulation. The simulated actuators are first-order, 20/(s+20), with deflection and rate limits (p. 1124). The paper says the sampling-interval sweep and K-gain sweep (Fig. 6, p. 1125) "verified" the trends with actuator limits included, and that high gain "degrades the performance for a > 8 and potentially leads to divergence" under actuator limits (p. 1125).

## (c) Assumptions and limitations

1. **Ideal actuators and perfect sensing in all proofs** (p. 1119). The paper states "the actuator dynamics, nonlinear limits of actuators, and the sensing issues will also be discussed in Sec. III.B", but these are handled through the singular-perturbation argument and simulation, not a theorem with numeric bounds (p. 1123).
2. **Internal dynamics must be ISS or only locally exponentially stable.** Theorem 1 requires ISS of η̇ = f_0(η, ξ); the paper says "the input-to-state stability of the internal dynamics is not easy to prove", so the aircraft example relies on local exponential stability of η̇ = f_d(η, 0, 0), checked by linearization (p. 1124). This is the Corollary 1 case, which is local.
3. **Restrictions on initial condition and perturbation size.** Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 require δ_ε (or δ_ε + d_ε) < ε* and a restricted initial condition (pp. 1120-1121).
4. **Ultimate boundedness, not asymptotic stability,** for the INDI perturbation case (Theorem 1; Corollary 2). The bounds are "could be conservative" because the cross term 2ξ^T P B_c δ(z;Δt) is bounded by its worst case (Remark after Prop. 1, p. 1121).
5. **Bounds are class-K functions with unspecified constants.** θ1, θ3, θ5, θ6, θ7, c1-c4, and ε* are not evaluated for the aircraft, so none of the theorems gives a number for Δt or for a tolerable B error.
6. **Square and relative-degree assumptions.** p = m, B(x) nonsingular (p. 1117). The model in Sec. IV uses four outputs (V_z, p, q, r) and four surfaces, with relative degree (1,1,1,1) and constant thrust (p. 1123).
7. **Numerical scope.** One rigid aircraft model with strip-theory aerodynamics, a 2-D von Kármán gust field (L_g = 762 m, σ = 3 m/s), and a 2000 Hz simulation step (pp. 1123-1124). The NDI/INDI comparison uses 100 Hz (p. 1126). No flight test and no multirotor model.
8. **Monte Carlo selection.** The 31 NDI failure cases (rms > 50 m/s or > 50 deg/s) are discarded before the box plots and Table 2 (p. 1128), so the NDI spread in Table 2 is conditional on not failing.
9. **Disturbance continuity argument.** The paper argues that because external disturbances are continuous, "lim_{Δt→0} ‖d‖₂ = 0" (p. 1118; repeated on p. 1121). The argument needs ‖Δd‖₂ → 0 instead, which the surrounding text uses ("when d ≠ 0, ∃Δt such that ‖Δd‖₂ < ‖d‖₂"). Likely a typo or extraction loss of Δ; see Unverified.
10. **Lag handling.** Angular acceleration is estimated by differentiating filtered rates, so the estimate is lagged. The paper says "synchronization errors still exist in practice" even when the input is filtered with the same filter (p. 1123). Suggested remedies (predictive filters [10], actuator compensators [27]) are listed as future work.

## (d) Implications for a PX4 rate-loop INDI design

These are my reading of the paper, not statements in it, unless attributed.

1. **Sample rate.** The paper's own rule of thumb for rigid aircraft is 100 Hz (p. 1121), and for quadrotor flight 512 Hz is cited as used in [13,14] (p. 1121). The bound on the perturbation is monotone in Δt: ultimate bounds fall as Δt falls (Fig. 6, p. 1125), but the paper also notes the gain falls off around Δt ≈ 0.12 s and that finer sampling "would impose higher requirements on the hardware" (p. 1125). A PX4 design should measure the achieved loop period and check that δ(z;Δt) stays within the bound used in the analysis.
2. **Effectiveness error (B̂).** The paper's only quantitative effectiveness-error result is B̂ = 1.3B at 100 Hz with the same gains for NDI and INDI, with NDI given the perfect α̂ = α. Under this mismatch, INDI RMS errors are 3.2 to 6.9 times smaller than NDI's (Table 1, "uncertain" rows, p. 1126; ratios recomputed from the table). The paper gives no bound on the tolerable B error. The condition for the INDI error term is ‖(B − B̂)Δu_indi‖ < ε* (Corollary 3), so the practical margin depends on how small Δu is per sample. For PX4 this means the effectiveness estimate should be checked at the actual loop period, not only in the nominal case.
3. **Filter and actuator lag.** The paper's stated margin is qualitative ("sufficiently small lags", p. 1123). No delay margin is quantified, and the proofs exclude actuator dynamics. A PX4 design should use its own lag analysis (gyro filter + angular-acceleration estimator + ESC/motor response), e.g., by the singular-perturbation margin argument of [26] or by simulation. The paper's simulation used a 50 ms-time-constant first-order actuator (20/(s+20)) with rate limits (p. 1124); this is an example, not a limit.
4. **Gain versus noise.** Higher K reduces ultimate bounds, but "high-gain control would also amplify measurement noise" and actuator limits constrain K (p. 1121, p. 1125). For a rate loop that differentiates gyro signals, this is a direct trade-off.
5. **Disturbance rejection structure.** The paper's disturbance result says external states see only Δd, while internal states see d̄ directly and cannot be reduced by sampling (Corollary 1, p. 1121). For a multirotor rate loop, the "internal" part is whatever dynamics the rate loop does not control (e.g., attitude/translational coupling, rotor drag). The paper does not analyze it for multirotors.
6. **Scope of the proof.** The results cover a square, relative-degree-known system with nonsingular B(x) and ISS or locally stable internal dynamics. A multirotor rate loop with four actuators and four rate channels matches this form, but actuator dynamics, sensor lag and saturation are outside the theorems.
