## Summary (5 lines)
1. Tracks position/yaw and their derivatives to snap, using flatness-based feedforward for body rate and angular acceleration (Abstract, p.1; Sec. II, p.3-4).
2. Linear acceleration and angular acceleration are tracked with INDI, i.e. incremental thrust and torque commands, so drag is not modelled (Abstract, p.1; Sec. I, p.2).
3. Snap (hence torque) is realised by closed-loop motor-speed control using optical encoders on each motor (p.2, Sec. I "Firstly").
4. Flight results: speeds to 12.9 m/s, accelerations to 2.1 g, RMS tracking error 6.6 cm, roughly 18 m x 7 m x 3 m volume; drag-plate and rope-pull robustness tests (Abstract, p.1; Sec. I, p.2).
5. No hardware baseline against PID or any other controller. The only non-INDI comparison is a simulated non-incremental law without integral action (Sec. IV-A, Figs. 7-10, pp.10-11). Internal ablations only: jerk/snap feedforward off (Table V, p.11) and linear vs nonlinear inversion (Sec. V-E, p.14, no numbers).

## (a) Control architecture

Cascade (Table II, p.6; block diagrams Figs. 2-4, pp.5-6):

| Component | Method | Output | Ref. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position/velocity | PD (cascaded) | a_c | Sec. III-A, eq. (17), p.6 |
| Linear acceleration and yaw | INDI | (tau b_z)_c -> T_c, xi_c | Sec. III-B, eqs. (18)-(26), pp.6-7 |
| Jerk/snap tracking | differential flatness | Omega_ref, dOmega_ref | Sec. II-B, eqs. (14)-(15), p.4 |
| Attitude and angular rate | PD, kinematic (no inertia) | dOmega_c | Sec. III-C, eqs. (27)-(28), p.7 |
| Angular acceleration | INDI | mu_c | Sec. III-D, eqs. (29)-(31), p.7 |
| Moment and thrust to motor speed | nonlinear inversion (Newton) | omega_c | Sec. III-E, eqs. (32)-(35), pp.7-8 |
| Motor speed control | integrative, ESC throttle | zeta | Sec. III-E, eq. (36), p.8 |

- **Outer loop is not INDI.** a_c = K_x(x_ref - x) + K_v(v_ref - v) + K_a(a_ref - a_f) + a_ref (eq. 17, p.6). The filtered measured acceleration a_f (eq. 16, p.4) enters only through the K_a term.
- **Thrust increment.** The INDI linear-acceleration law is derived from the external-force estimate f_ext = m(a_f - (tau b_z)_f - g i_z) (eq. 18, p.6). Substituting gives a = tau b_z - (tau b_z)_f + a_f (eq. 19), and the thrust-vector increment is (tau b_z)_c = (tau b_z)_f + a_c - a_f (eq. 20, p.6). The magnitude is T_c = -m ||(tau b_z)_c||_2 (eq. 21, p.7). The authors state the incremental form "eliminates the need for integral action anywhere in the control design" (Sec. III-B, p.6).
- **Attitude increment.** Computed in two steps, both relative to the current attitude (Sec. III-B, p.7): (i) the minimum rotation aligning -b_z with the commanded thrust direction (eqs. 22-23); (ii) a rotation about b_z to satisfy psi_ref (eqs. 24-25; xi_psi chooses psi_ref or psi_ref + pi by minimum rotation). Final increment xi_c = xi_bar_c o xi_psi (eq. 26). The authors note singularities at (-b_z)^b_c = i_z and at n_2 = 0 and say computing relative to current attitude "moves these singularities far away from the nominal trajectory" (p.7).
- **Attitude PD.** Error angles xi_e from the quaternion error (eq. 27, p.7). Angular-acceleration command dOmega_c = K_xi xi_e + K_Omega(Omega_ref - Omega_f) + dOmega_ref (eq. 28, p.7). Authors argue the attitude loop uses no inertia matrix J, so it is "vehicle-independent" (Sec. III-C, p.7).
- **Angular-acceleration INDI.** External moment estimate mu_ext = J dOmega_f - mu_f + Omega_f x J Omega_f (eq. 29, p.7). Then dOmega = dOmega_f + J^-1(mu - mu_f) (eq. 30). The gyroscopic term difference is neglected as "second-order" (p.7). Commanded moment mu_c = mu_f + J(dOmega_c - dOmega_f) (eq. 31, p.7).
- **Inner motor loop.** Thrust and moments are mapped to motor speeds by nonlinear inversion of eq. (5) (mu; T)= G1 omega^{o2} + G2 domega (eq. 5, p.3), with a first-order motor model domega = tau_m^-1 (omega_c - omega) (eq. 32, p.7). Equation (33) is solved numerically, e.g. Newton's method (p.8). Saturation is handled by first moving mu_zc (eq. 34, p.8) and then clipping (p.8). Throttle: zeta = p(omega_c) + K_Iomega integral(omega_c - omega) dt (eq. 36, p.8).

## Sec. IV (response analysis, linearised; feeds sections (e) and (f))

- **Linearisation.** Hover linearisation with kG = 8 omega_0 l_x k_tau (eq. 42 text, p.9), giving omega_c = omega_f + J_yy k_G^-1 (alpha_c - alpha_f) (eq. 42, p.9). Non-incremental counterpart: theta_c,NI = -a_x,ref/g, omega_c,NI = (J_yy/k_G) alpha_c (eq. 43, p.9).
- **Closed-loop pitch-acceleration dynamics** (eq. 44, p.9): with ideal modelling, the closed-loop angular-acceleration dynamics "are solely determined by the motor dynamics [18]" and so "the aggressiveness of trajectories that can be tracked is theoretically limited by only the bandwidth of the motor response", stated to hold also for the non-incremental controller (Sec. IV-A, p.9).
- **Disturbance rejection.** Disturbance moment counteraction depends on H(s) and M(s) (eq. 45, p.9); "the ability to reject disturbances is limited by the bandwidth of both the LPFs and the motors" (Sec. IV-A, p.9). Non-incremental: the disturbance "propagates undamped" (p.9).
- **Modelling error.** With J_yy/k_G replaced by nominal values and Delta defined in eq. (46) (p.10), the incremental loop gives alpha/alpha_c = Delta M / ((Delta-1) H M + 1) (eq. 47, p.10); the non-incremental loop gives alpha/alpha_c,NI = Delta M (eq. 48, p.10): Delta "acts as a simple gain" in the non-incremental controller (p.10).
- **Integral action.** Non-incremental controller needs integral action to null steady-state error from force/moment disturbances; "This is not necessary in the case of INDI" (Sec. IV-A, p.10). Same claim as in Sec. III-B (p.6).
- **Simulation setup (Fig. 7, Sec. IV-A, p.10):** platform-independent gains of Table III, 2nd-order Butterworth LPF with cut-off 188.5 rad/s (30 Hz), first-order motor model (eq. 32) with tau_m = 20 ms. Table III (p.10): K_x = diag(18, 18, 13.5), K_v = diag(7.8, 7.8, 5.9), K_a = diag(0.5, 0.5, 0.3), K_xi = diag(175, 175, 82), a gain labelled "K_xi-dot" = diag(19.5, 19.5, 19.2) [label garbled in text layer; text uses K_Omega, so [reconstructed]].
- **Feedforward zeros (eq. 50, Sec. IV-C, p.11):** the jerk/snap feedforward terms add two zeros that "in combination with the LPF ... act essentially as a lead compensator" (p.11). Simulated with/without jerk-snap tracking (Fig. 10, p.11).
- **Scope of Sec. IV.** Linearised analysis; authors state the observations "also apply to the full, nonlinear dynamics" (Sec. IV intro, p.8) and validate it only through flights in Sec. V (p.8).

## (b) Actuator-state source

- **Source.** Motor speeds from one optical encoder per motor: "detecting the passage of stripes on a reflective strip attached to the motor hub" (Sec. III-E, p.8; Fig. 5 caption, p.8). Described as "high-rate, accurate, lightweight, and unintrusive" (p.8).
- **Use in the loop.** Measured speeds feed the specific-thrust and moment estimates tau = f(omega) and mu_f via eq. (5) (Sec. III-B, p.6; Sec. III-D, p.7). "The measured motor speed signal omega remains unfiltered here to minimize phase lag" (Sec. III-E, p.8, in the throttle/feedback discussion around eq. 36).
- **Filtering of IMU signals.** "A low-pass filter (LPF) is required to alleviate the effects of noise, e.g., airframe vibrations" (Sec. III, p.5). Angular acceleration is obtained by numerical differentiation of the measured rate, then filtered (Sec. III, p.5). Fig. 4 (p.6) places LPF blocks on the IMU-derived signals and on the encoder-derived (omega^{o2})_f and its derivative, feeding tau_f, mu_f and T_f [block labels partly garbled in the text layer, reconstructed].
- **Synchronisation.** "Identical LPFs must be used to ensure that equal phase lag is incurred by acceleration and thrust measurements [18]" (Sec. III-B, p.6). Acceleration is rotated to the inertial frame before filtering, "because the external force in the inertial reference frame is assumed to be slow-changing relative to the LPF dynamics" (Sec. III-B, p.6).
- **Flight filter.** "The motor speed and IMU measurements are low-pass filtered using a software second-order Butterworth filter with cutoff frequency 188.5 rad/s (30 Hz)" (Sec. V-A, p.12). The same filter is stated for both sensor families; no separate parameters are given for motor speed and IMU. Encoders sampled "at approximately 5000 Hz", IMU at 2000 Hz, control at 2000 Hz (Sec. V-A, pp.11-12).
- **Apparent tension (flag).** Sec. III-E (p.8) says the throttle-feedback speed "remains unfiltered", while Sec. V-A (p.12) says motor speed is filtered. Reading: the first refers to the throttle loop, the second to the inversion inputs. Not resolved by the text. [unverified]
- **Synchronisation: stated vs not stated.** Stated: identical LPFs for acceleration and thrust measurements, to give equal phase lag (Sec. III-B, p.6); acceleration rotated to inertial frame before filtering (p.6). Not stated: how ~5000 Hz encoder samples are time-aligned with 2000 Hz IMU samples (no resampling or timestamp description found); how d(omega)/dt for the G2 term in eq. (5) is obtained (numerical differentiation or model) [unverified].
- **Motor time constant.** tau_m = 20 ms in simulation (Sec. IV-A, p.10). The flight value is not stated in the text read. Mismatch of tau_m and Delta t "may result in fast yaw oscillations" (Sec. V-E, p.14).

## (c) Control effectiveness model and its identification

- **Model.** (mu; T) = G1 omega^{o2} + G2 domega (eq. 5, p.3). G1 (eq. 6, p.3) depends on moment arms l_x, l_y, thrust coefficient k_tau and torque coefficient k_muz; G2 (eq. 7, p.3) on rotor+propeller inertia J_rz. The text says the motor contribution to total angular momentum "may be neglected" (p.3).
- **Linearised pitch effectiveness.** k_G = 8 omega_0 l_x k_tau (p.9, after eq. 42), "with the factor 4 due to the number of motors" (p.9, eq. 41 text).
- **Throttle map.** p(.) "obtained by regression analysis of static test data", with integral action "to account for changes in this relation due to decreasing battery voltage" (Sec. III-E, p.8).
- **Identification.** "We obtained control effectiveness data from static tests" (Sec. V-A, p.12). Platform parameters the controller needs: m, J, tau_m, G1, G2, the gain and the polynomial fit (Sec. V-A, p.12). Not found in the text read: the test procedure, the identified values of k_tau, k_muz, l_x, l_y, J_rz, J, m, and any fit error. [gap]
- **Why it matters.** Static data "may not be representative for the entire flight envelope" (Sec. IV-B, p.10). The incremental loop is shown to tolerate large effectiveness error Delta in simulation (eqs. 46-48, Figs. 8-9, pp.10-11).
- **Portability claim.** On a different quadcopter (different dynamics, sensors and propulsion), "required no changes to controller algorithms or gains. After updating only the aforementioned platform-specific parameters, the controller performed without loss of tracking accuracy" (Sec. V-A, p.12). No second-platform data in the text read. [unverified]
- **Inversion choice.** Nonlinear inversion of eq. (5) with motor lag (eq. 33, p.8) vs linearised inversion (eq. 52, p.14). Flight: "does not lead to significant differences in tracking performance", but the linearised form can cause yaw oscillation when tau_m and Delta t differ (Sec. V-E, p.14). No numbers given.

## (e) Limitations and failure modes the authors mention

The paper has no dedicated limitations section in the text read. Items below are stated in the body.

1. **Motor saturation.** Infeasible motor commands are handled by reallocating the z-moment, then reducing thrust, then clipping (Sec. III-E, p.8). In flight, a drag-plate yaw moment "causes motor speed saturation and very large momentary yaw tracking error" (Sec. V-D, p.14; Table V(iii) max yaw 51.7 deg).
2. **Yaw oscillation with linearised inversion** if tau_m and Delta t differ greatly (Sec. V-E, p.14).
3. **Steady-state force offset.** "small steady-state value due to discrepancy between true and estimated thrust" in the estimated disturbance force (Sec. V-F, p.14; Fig. 17, p.15).
4. **Bandwidth ceiling.** Tracking aggressiveness "theoretically limited by only the bandwidth of the motor response" (Sec. IV-A, p.9). Disturbance rejection "limited by the bandwidth of both the LPFs and the motors" (Sec. IV-A, p.9).
5. **Attitude-increment singularities** at (-b_z)^b_c = i_z and at n_2 = 0, moved away from the nominal trajectory by working relative to the current attitude (Sec. III-B, p.7).
6. **Neglected terms.** Gyroscopic term difference "neglected" as second-order (Sec. III-D, p.7).
7. **Encoder dependence.** Snap tracking "requires direct control of body torque, which we achieve using closed-loop motor speed control based on measurements from optical encoders attached to the motors" (Abstract, p.1). No fallback without encoders is analysed.
8. **Vehicle parameters still needed.** Controller "requires several platform-specific parameters" (Sec. V-A, p.12); the claim of "few vehicle-specific parameters" is at Sec. IV-B, p.10.
9. **Linear analysis.** Sec. IV analyses linearised dynamics; the authors say the observations "also apply to the full, nonlinear dynamics" but are "validated ... using real-life flights in Section V" (Sec. IV intro, p.8).
10. **Motion-capture latency.** 18 ms, compensated by IMU propagation (Sec. V-A, p.11). No sensitivity analysis is given.
11. **Envelope.** Indoor, one vehicle, peak 12.9 m/s and 2.12 g (Sec. V-A, V-B, pp.11-13).

## (f) Implications for a PX4 rate-loop INDI, esp. without motor-speed feedback

What the paper relies on:
- The incremental moment command uses measured actuator state: mu_c = mu_f + J(dOmega_c - dOmega_f) (eq. 31, p.7), where mu_f is built from measured motor speeds through eq. (5) and LPF (Sec. III-D, p.7). Thrust increment similarly uses (tau b_z)_f from measured speeds (eqs. 18, 20, p.6).
- The closed-loop and disturbance results depend on H(s) and M(s) (eqs. 44-45, p.9). The paper does not test what happens when the actuator state is replaced by a model.

Consequences for a project with no motor-speed feedback (for example PX4 SITL by default, per the project brief):
- **Paper has no answer.** No experiment or simulation in the text uses a modelled or commanded actuator state in place of measured speed. Any claim that INDI works without encoders is outside this paper. [gap]
- **Inference (not in the paper).** A natural substitute is a model estimate omega_hat driven by the commanded speed through the first-order motor model of eq. (32): omega_hat' = (omega_c - omega_hat)/tau_m. Then mu_f and T_f are computed from omega_hat. The error of this estimate enters the increment directly, so the rejection analysis of eqs. (45)-(47) would need redoing with actuator-model error rather than the inertia/effectiveness error Delta analysed in the paper. The paper does not address this. [inference]
- **Inference.** Gyro-derived angular acceleration and accelerometer-derived acceleration remain measured, so disturbance correction through the incremental term still reacts to the real vehicle; the lost part is the actuator-side reference. [inference]
- **Filter matching.** Paper's principle: identical LPFs on the two signals subtracted in the increment, for equal phase lag (Sec. III-B, p.6). A PX4 port would need the gyro-derivative filter and the actuator-estimate filter matched in the same way. [inference from p.6]
- **Parameters to identify.** m, J, tau_m, G1 and G2 (i.e. k_tau, k_muz, l_x, l_y, J_rz), plus the throttle polynomial with integral correction (Sec. III-E p.8; Sec. V-A p.12). The paper gives no procedure for this (see (c)).
- **Sample rates and filter.** Paper: 2000 Hz control, 2nd-order Butterworth at 30 Hz on motor speed and IMU (Sec. V-A, pp.11-12). Choice of 30 Hz is not justified in the text. Phase lag of the filter should be compared with tau_m = 20 ms before reuse. [inference]
- **PID comparison.** The paper does not provide the comparison needed for the project's question. Any statement that INDI beats PID needs data from other sources or from our own tests.
- **Yaw.** Because of the Table V label issue, yaw numbers should not be used as like-for-like comparisons.
