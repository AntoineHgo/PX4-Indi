## Reading

Lower yaw gains (D-r3) improved J from 1.197 to 1.055, but PID (0.96) still wins under the frozen cost. The best
INDI candidate tracks better than PID on every tuning scenario except rate_step_roll (+3.5 %) and uses equal or
less effort everywhere except step_yaw. The step_yaw effort penalty alone is 0.2 x (5.2 - 1) / 5 ~ 0.17 of J;
without it c13 would score ~0.89.

`bench/yaw_step_effort.py` on step_yaw seed 1 (c13 vs PID defaults), window [t_step - 0.2, t_step + 2] s:

| | motor-rate RMS | along yaw | peak r_sp | peak yaw torque | peak K_Y r_sp / G1_Y |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| INDI c13 | 4.70 | 8.89 | 2.44 | 0.78 | 0.83 |
| PID | 0.79 | 1.53 | 2.15 | 0.33 | |

The yaw torque no longer saturates (r2: 1.0), but it still jumps: the setpoint term K_Y r_sp / G1_Y (0.83) passes
unfiltered into the increment, so the yaw torque, and the motor commands, step at the setpoint step. PID smooths
the same setpoint step with its 2 Hz yaw torque output filter (MC_YAW_TQ_CUTOFF default), which acts on the whole
yaw command. The motor-rate metric (sum of |du/dt|) is dominated by that step. This is structural, not a gain
issue: no gain in the r3 box removes it without giving up yaw tracking.
