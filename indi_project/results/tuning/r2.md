"Implied effort ratio" is `1 + (J - mean ratio) / 0.2`, i.e. it assumes
every (scenario, seed) paid the effort penalty; it is an estimate of the
average motor-rate RMS relative to PID, not a measured value.

Reading:
- Same picture as r1, slightly better: INDI tracks better than PID
  defaults on hover, attitude steps and yaw steps (12-18 % lower primary
  metric), and equal on rate pulses; but its motor-rate effort is still
  about 2.3-2.6x PID's, and the frozen cost penalises that more than the
  tracking gain. PID wins round 2 under the frozen cost (0.958 vs 1.150).
- Best INDI improved J by only 1.5 % over r1 (1.167 -> 1.150). Two rounds
  of sampling the gain space have not closed the effort gap: it is not a
  gain-tuning problem but a noise-transmission one (session 9 traced the
  hover effort to the yaw path; the yaw cut-off in r2 is 2.6-4.6 Hz versus
  PID's 2 Hz torque low-pass).
- Next: measure effort per axis for c11 (`bench/effort_axes.py`) before any
  round 3; if yaw still dominates, test YAW_CUT at 2 Hz (PID's value) as a
  matched-filter control; if roll/pitch dominate, look at the a_f filter
  cut-off (gyro-derivative noise). Do not change the cost function after
  the fact: the effort penalty was frozen before INDI flew.
